Hiring a WordPress developer in Chandigarh is less about finding someone who “knows WordPress” and more about finding someone who can turn your business requirements into a maintainable site your team can update safely. WordPress powers a huge range of projects—from brochure sites to complex content hubs—so the hiring bar should match the risk of your brief.

This article walks through a practical hiring process: writing the brief, screening skills, reading proposals and setting up delivery. ## Start with a brief that prevents wasted quotes

Before you message developers, write a one-page brief covering:

- Business goal (leads, sales, education, support)
- Primary audiences and key pages
- Must-have integrations (CRM, WhatsApp, payments, booking)
- Who will edit content after launch
- Brand assets available now versus later
- Languages, locations and compliance notes
- Target launch window and hard deadlines

Attach examples of sites you like *and* explain what you like about them. “Make it like this” without specifics produces guesswork. Mention pages that must be migrated, URLs that must keep ranking and forms that must not break during cutover.

> A clear brief attracts serious proposals. Vague briefs attract vague prices—and messy change requests later.

## Which WordPress skills to verify

Not every WordPress developer is interchangeable. Match skills to work type.

**Theme and template work.** Confirm they can build reusable patterns, not one-off page designs that break when editors duplicate content. Ask how they structure headers, footers, blog templates and landing layouts.

**Plugin judgment.** Experienced developers choose fewer, better-supported plugins and avoid stacking overlapping tools. Ask what they would refuse to install and why.

**Performance and security basics.** Hosting choices, caching strategy, image handling, update discipline and backup cadence matter as much as visual design. Use [WordPress speed optimization checklist](/blog/wordpress-speed-optimization-checklist) as a shared reference during kickoff.

**WooCommerce or membership experience.** If you sell online, verify checkout customization, tax/shipping logic and payment testing. The companion piece [WooCommerce developer in Chandigarh](/blog/woocommerce-developer-chandigarh) goes deeper on store-specific hiring.

**Custom development.** ACF, custom post types, REST API work and light React integrations show up often on serious business sites. Ask for examples where custom code was necessary versus where native features were enough.

## Screening portfolio work the right way

Live sites beat screenshots. For each sample, ask:

1. What problem did the client need solved?
2. What did you personally build?
3. How do editors update the site today?
4. What would you improve if you rebuilt it now?

Look for clean admin experience, not only homepage polish. Open an interior page, a blog post and a form confirmation state if possible. Check mobile navigation and load feel on a mid-range phone connection.

## Proposal checks that protect your budget

Request proposals against the same brief. Score them on:

- Template and page inventory
- Plugin and hosting assumptions
- Content migration plan
- SEO and redirect plan
- Training format (live session, Loom, docs)
- Support window after launch
- Payment milestones tied to deliverables

Be wary of ultra-cheap “unlimited page” packages. Unlimited usually means shallow templates and slow communication. Prefer defined scope with a clear path for extras.

## Interview flow that surfaces real capability

A useful interview sequence:

1. Candidate restates your brief
2. Candidate outlines risks and open questions
3. You review one past project together
4. You discuss content workflow and training
5. You agree on communication tools and update rhythm

Ask scenario questions: broken contact form after plugin update, slow blog archive, redirect map for a domain migration, or editors accidentally breaking layout. Strong answers include diagnosis steps and prevention habits.

Also ask how they handle staging. Building directly on production is a common source of downtime. Staging plus backup before updates should be non-negotiable for business sites.

## Timeline realities for WordPress projects

A simple marketing site can move quickly when content is ready. Multi-language sites, memberships, migrations and custom booking flows take longer. Build a timeline that includes:

- Discovery and sitemap
- Design or design-system alignment
- Development on staging
- Content entry and QA
- Training
- Launch and monitoring

Content is usually the critical path. Assign an internal owner for copy and images in week one. Freeze major content changes before final QA so you are not testing a moving target.

## Contracts, access and handoff essentials

Before work starts, clarify:

- Who owns the domain, hosting and theme code
- Admin user roles and password handling
- License ownership for premium plugins/themes
- What happens if either side pauses the project
- Warranty coverage for bugs versus new feature requests

At handoff, you should receive staging credentials (or confirmation they are closed), production access, documentation of custom fields and a short maintenance recommendation. You should never be locked out of your own property.

## Content migration and SEO continuity

If you already have a site, migration quality often matters more than visual novelty. Ask your WordPress developer to inventory URLs, map redirects and preserve metadata for pages that already attract traffic. Confirm who enters content into the new templates and how media will be compressed during import.

Request a staging checklist that includes form tests, 404 spot checks and analytics verification before DNS cutover. Businesses that skip this step frequently blame WordPress for problems that were actually launch process gaps. A careful migration plan is one of the strongest signals you hired the right partner.
