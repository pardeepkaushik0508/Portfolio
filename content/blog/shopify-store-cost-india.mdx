Businesses asking how much it costs to build a Shopify store in India usually want a single number. That number does not exist in a useful form. A ten-SKU boutique on a lightly customized theme is a different project from a multi-collection catalogue with migration, custom sections, and integrated logistics. This article breaks the budget into parts you can estimate honestly—without inventing industry-wide averages or promising a fixed price that ignores scope.

For wider website budgeting principles, see [professional website cost](/blog/professional-website-cost). For platform choice, start with [Shopify vs WooCommerce](/blog/shopify-vs-woocommerce).

## Cost layer 1: Shopify plan and commerce fees

Shopify charges a subscription based on plan. Payment processing fees depend on whether you use Shopify-managed payments where available or third-party gateways, and on your plan’s rates. Those fees are operating costs, not one-time build costs, but they belong in the same spreadsheet as your website budget.

Factor in domains, professional email, and any Shopify markets or add-ons you truly need. Ignore upsells that do not match your catalogue yet.

> Budget the store as a system: platform fees, build effort, apps, and maintenance. A low build quote with an expensive, unclear monthly stack is not a bargain.

## Cost layer 2: Theme and design

Themes are either free, paid one-time purchases, or custom-built. Paid themes can be good value when their section library matches your merchandising. Custom design work—brand-new UI, unique product templates, campaign systems—costs more because it is labour, not a licence.

Understand what [Shopify theme customization](/blog/shopify-theme-customization) includes before you compare quotes. Editor-only polish is not the same as Liquid sections, variant UX, and performance passes.

## Cost layer 3: Custom development and integrations

This is where India project quotes diverge most. Custom [Shopify sections](/blog/custom-shopify-sections), a tailored [product variant picker](/blog/shopify-product-variant-picker), checkout-adjacent extensibility, ERP sync, and wholesale flows require development time. So do migrations—see [migrate a store to Shopify](/blog/migrate-store-to-shopify).

Ask proposers to separate: theme setup, custom sections, migration/redirects, app configuration, and QA. Bundled “Shopify package” pricing that hides those lines makes change requests painful later.

Decide early where apps suffice versus custom build using [Shopify apps vs custom development](/blog/shopify-apps-vs-custom-development). Apps add monthly cost; custom work adds upfront cost and some retainer for upkeep.

## Cost layer 4: Apps and third-party tools

Reviews, subscriptions, loyalty, search, SMS, and accounting connectors each add subscription fees—often billed in USD. Five “inexpensive” apps can exceed a theme’s annual impact. Install deliberately. Revisit the stack after launch.

## Cost layer 5: Content, photography, and launch ops

Budgets frequently omit product photography, copywriting, size charts, and policy pages. They also omit redirect mapping and Search Console work from the [technical SEO checklist for Shopify stores](/blog/technical-seo-checklist-shopify). Those items affect whether the store earns money, not only whether it looks finished.

Conversion readiness—clear product pages, trust, speed—belongs in the build, not only in ads spend. Use the [Shopify conversion optimization checklist](/blog/shopify-conversion-optimization) and [Shopify speed optimization](/blog/shopify-speed-optimization) as scope references when comparing proposals. Campaign templates should follow [high-converting landing page](/blog/high-converting-landing-page) thinking if paid traffic is part of the plan.

## Rough project shapes (not price promises)

**Lean launch.** Small catalogue, paid or free theme, light customization, essential apps, careful setup. Best when you need to validate demand quickly and will invest in sections later.

**Growth storefront.** Custom sections, stronger product templates, performance pass, SEO hygiene, selective apps. Typical for brands that already sell and need a durable storefront.

**Migration or complex catalogue.** Data cleanup, redirects, theme rebuild, app replacement, extended QA. Highest coordination cost; cheapest path is rarely the shortest quote.

Exact INR figures change with scope and who you hire. Treat any public “average cost in India” claim with skepticism unless it states inclusions. Evaluate proposals against deliverables.

## Ongoing costs after launch

Expect subscription renewals, app fees, occasional theme updates, seasonal landing work, and bug fixes. A small monthly retainer for storefront support often costs less than emergency fixes during festival traffic. Technical SEO and content updates continue; see also the [technical SEO checklist for modern websites](/blog/technical-seo-checklist-modern-websites).

Write a brief: SKUs, languages, markets, must-have features, apps to keep, migration sources, and examples you like. Ask for staged milestones. Clarify who enters catalogue data. ## What to put in an RFP or brief

Include:

- Approximate SKU and variant counts  
- Languages and selling markets  
- Whether this is a new store or a migration  
- Must-have apps and integrations  
- Design references and brand assets status  
- Who writes copy and shoots products  
- Success metrics for launch (not vanity)  
- Support expectations for the first month  

Ask responders to quote optional add-ons separately: custom variant UX, section library, speed pass, and redirect mapping. That structure makes bids comparable. Guidance in [hire a Shopify developer in Chandigarh](/blog/hire-shopify-developer-chandigarh) helps you evaluate people, not only prices.

## Hidden costs teams forget

- Payment gateway KYC delays that push launch dates  
- App currencies and GST treatment on subscriptions  
- Rework when catalogue data arrives late or incomplete  
- Festival-season change requests without a change budget  
- Recovering from a cheap theme that cannot support your product UX  

Build a contingency line—commonly a portion of the build budget—for surprises. Migrations need more contingency than greenfield stores.

## Choosing between Shopify and alternatives on cost alone

Do not pick WooCommerce only because hosting looks cheaper on a shared plan, and do not pick Shopify only because a theme demo looks polished. Compare total cost of ownership across a year, including people time. ## Paying for quality signals, not buzzwords

In proposals, prefer concrete deliverables: number of custom sections, templates in scope, performance pass on product pages, redirect count estimate, training session, and post-launch support window. Phrases like “SEO friendly” or “premium UX” without artifacts are weak signals.

Ask for relevant storefront references. Theme craft visible in projects such as [Get Boldify](/#more-projects) and [Talwar Sons](/#more-projects) is easier to evaluate than slide decks. Browse [more projects](/#more-projects) and [services](/#services) while you compare options. If two quotes are similar in price, prefer the one that schedules staging QA and a documented section handoff—those habits protect the investment described throughout [Shopify theme customization](/blog/shopify-theme-customization).

## Closing budgeting advice

Price the platform, the theme labour, the integrations, the apps, and the content ops as one model. Choose a lean launch only when scope is truly lean. For growth or migration projects, fund storefront craft and redirects properly—those line items decide whether your Shopify store in India is an asset or an expensive experiment.
